By April 2020, every RSM tax office in the country was racing to identify which of their clients qualified for CARES Act subsidies. They were all doing it manually. They were all doing it differently. They were all running out of time.

## The bottleneck wasn't the math. It was the coordination.

Tax leaders knew how to calculate eligibility — but the rules differed sharply by entity type (partnerships, S-corps, C-corps, sole proprietorships, LLCs, and trusts each carried their own carve-outs and exclusions), and each of RSM's five regions had its own conventions, edge cases, and authoritative voices on what to count.

Encoding the calc in software was the easy part. The actual project was getting 5 regional tax leaderships to agree on shared definitions, then convincing 90 offices to standardize their data inputs against those definitions — fast, before the relief window closed.

## How it shipped

![From tax codes to targeted relief — Dig, Translate, Size](/images/projects/cares-act-data-diagram.png)

1. **Convened the experts.** Sat with regional tax leaders across all five RSM regions to extract the eligibility logic for each of the six entity types. Subject-matter experts labeled the data; my job was to encode their judgment, not invent it.
2. **Built six pipeline branches in Alteryx**, one per entity type, with regional carve-outs treated as parameters. A partnership's K-1 distributions look nothing like an S-corp's wage base, so a one-size-fits-all schema would have collapsed under its own exceptions. Six branches kept each entity-type's nuances intact.
3. **Started in one office** to prove the pattern, then scaled to 90. The harder problem at this stage wasn't the pipeline — it was data hygiene. The firm's reporting tools were thin, so most office data had to be manually keyed against the new spec. Each office adoption was its own coordination project.
4. **Built Power BI dashboards** so tax partners could explore results by region, entity type, and subsidy program — and target the highest-impact upsell opportunities themselves.

## What it added up to

- **$200M of economic impact** identified across 20,000 client accounts
- **6 entity types · 5 regions · 90 offices** standardized on one shared calc instead of 90 parallel ones
- **~10,000 hours of manual interpretation work** absorbed by the framework
- The Innovation Lab earned firm-wide recognition for positioning RSM as a proactive advisor during an international crisis

## What I took from it

The encoded calc was maybe 10% of the work. The other 90% was getting five regional teams to agree on shared definitions, then earning trust from 90 offices one at a time — starting with the smallest visible win.
