A Bakersfield restaurant owner was sitting on a $20K Employee Retention Credit she'd never heard of. Her tax preparer hadn't either. The relief window was closing in 11 weeks.

She wasn't unusual. Tens of thousands of small businesses across the state were eligible for CARES Act relief and didn't know it — because eligibility wasn't sitting in any database. It was hiding inside tax codes nobody had time to read carefully.

## The real bottleneck wasn't compute

State agencies could afford the engineering. What they couldn't afford was the **interpretation time**. Eligibility for ERC, Round 2 PPP, SETC, and a dozen other programs lived inside specific combinations of Form 941 line items, NAICS classifications, and PPP forgiveness flags — across four legacy systems that didn't speak to each other.

Compute was cheap. Judgment was scarce. And the window was closing.

## How we did it

Three phases — the diagram says it cleanest:

![From tax codes to targeted relief — Dig, Translate, Size](/images/projects/cares-act-data-diagram.png)

**Dig** pulled and reconciled records into a single 100K+ candidate pool. **Translate** turned cryptic tax-code patterns into plain-English eligibility verdicts, each defensible against the underlying statute. **Size** tiered the eligible accounts by expected relief value so administrators could sequence outreach by impact-per-contact.

The Alteryx and Power BI work was the easy part. The hard part was the rule mappings — the line where regulatory language stops and a yes/no decision starts. That was the consulting work.

## What it added up to

- **20,000 accounts** verified against statute, not just keyword-matched
- **$200M of addressable relief**, sized by tier — so admins worked the highest-impact outreach first
- **~10,000 hours of manual interpretation** absorbed by the framework
- Delivered before the window closed

## What I took from it

Most data projects fail not because the pipelines break, but because nobody translates the rules into action while there's still time to act.

You can't automate judgment. You can only scale it — once you've made it.
