## Business Context

During COVID, RSM's tax Business Unit had an opportunity to help its clients access emergency relief funds. 90 offices were running the eligibility calculation by hand, separately. At 20K client accounts and roughly 30 minutes each, that was 10K hours of interpretation work spread across the firm, with no two offices interpreting the rules the same way.

The Innovation Lab was a brand new internal team chartered to make RSM's 13,000+ consultants more productive. I was the 2nd hire, working with Zackery, the team's Director. My job: build the eligibility logic from RSM's five regional tax leaderships, prove it in one office, and scale it to 90. 

## Role

| Area | What I owned |
|---|---|
| Specs | Shared spreadsheet for regional tax leaders to communicate the business logic & calculations |
| Pipeline | 6 Alteryx branches to handle nuances between each entity type |
| Pilot Delivery | Ran the first office end-to-end against real messy data, wrote a playbook the next 90 offices |
| Rollout | Ran the pipelines across all offices, Build Power BI dashboards shared with regional and office leaders |

## Act 1: Tax Code to Pipeline

Six entity types and five regional tax leaderships meant a lot of ground to cover. Sitting next to a tax expert and asking questions would have taken weeks. Instead, I asked the tax leaders to build the calculation in Excel themselves and send it over. The spreadsheets became a shared artifact and spec that kept us on the same page.

Each entity type had its own calc, so the pipeline ran six branches in Alteryx, one per type: partnerships, S-corps, C-corps, sole proprietorships, LLCs, trusts. A couple of states had wrinkles we handled separately. A mapping document tracked the state differences so the pipelines stayed aligned with how the tax leaders read the rules.

## Act 2: Chicago Pilot & Playbook

I picked one office (Chicago) to run first. The partners there were willing and bought in. The point was to prove the framework on real data before asking 89 more offices to invest the same data-keying effort.

The real data was messier than the spreadsheets suggested. Offices exported what they could from their tax software, then hand-keyed the fields the software didn't surface. Labels were inconsistent across accounts and across regions, same concept under different names, so the pipeline had to map them to a single schema. Joining the exported data with the hand-keyed fields was its own step before the pipeline could run.

Per-account time dropped from 30 minutes to seconds, and the pipeline surfaced a concrete dollar figure of CARES opportunity to take into client conversations.

It also produced the playbook. Training process, drop location in the shared drive, help channels for questions, escalation paths to the regional tax leaders. Every office after the pilot got the playbook, not a custom rollout.

## Act 3: Scaling to 90 Offices

The regional tax leaders had built the specs, watched the pilot work, and had a story to take into their own regions. They drove adoption inside their regions. The unit of work was partner conversations, not code commits. Each office adoption was its own coordination project: partner sponsorship, data re-keying against the spec, a debrief. The rollout was sequenced more like a sales pipeline than a software rollout.

My job was operations and distribution, not heroics. I ran the Alteryx pipelines over the network across the offices once their data landed. I handed Power BI dashboards to the regional and office leaders so partners could slice the output themselves by region, entity type, and subsidy program.

## Results

|---|---|
| 20,000 client accounts identified | Surfaced the CARES-eligible population across the firm so partners had a target list while the opportunity was fresh |
| $200M of CARES opportunity sized | Gave partners a concrete dollar figure to anchor their client conversations |
| 30 minutes per account dropped to seconds | The framework absorbed roughly 10,000 hours of manual interpretation work |

CARES was the Innovation Lab's first big win. A brand new internal team needed a track record before it would earn the next project, and the work delivered the proof and the credibility the team needed. The team is still inside RSM, now running as the firm's Enterprise AI practice. The operating model from CARES (encode judgment in a shared spec, prove it in one office, let the regional owners drive adoption) is how the practice still works.

## My Takeaways

- **Spec by spreadsheet works for SME-heavy domains.** Have the experts build the calc in their own tool. Translate from the artifact, not from the conversation.
- **Pick the pilot office for trust, not for clean data.** A willing, high-status office becomes the story the next offices follow. The clean-data version of this pilot would have been less useful.
- **Self-serve dashboards beat central routing at multi-office scale.** A team triaging accounts to ninety offices is a bottleneck on day one.
