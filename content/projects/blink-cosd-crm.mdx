B-LINK was a two-year Dynamics 365 implementation for the County of San Diego, connecting three agencies into one shared benefits-fraud investigation system for 2,000+ case managers.

I led the project as PM and delivery lead, owning the roadmap, client demos, scope management, engineering coordination, and rollout planning from kickoff through launch.

The engagement became the largest West Region CRM project in RSM’s practice at the time and helped establish the delivery model we reused on later public-sector CRM work.

## Role

My work included both client-facing delivery and internal execution.

## System

B-LINK covered the full public benefits lifecycle: eligibility through data integrations with state systems, field investigations on mobile apps for BPAI investigators, and adjudication of cross-agency evidence and fraud determinations.

![B-LINK architecture: three agencies routed through SSO and role-based access into a shared D365 CE tenant running the Eligibility → Investigation → Adjudication workflow, with Azure Functions syncing state databases, custom certificate-based document signing + government storage handoff, and agency-scoped Power BI reporting on top](/images/projects/blink-crm-diagram5.png)

- **Stack:**
  - D365 CE tenant with bespoke plugins
  - D365 Portals (SAML/SSO) for external users
  - Azure Functions for state-system integrations (e.g. CMIPS)
  - Custom Certificate Signature integration for doc-management
  - Power BI for agency-scoped reporting
  - Azure DevOps for backlog management



## Key Decisions

Three agencies had never shared a system, cross-agency adjudication needed evidence to flow across boundaries, and a key dependency lived inside our prime contractor's other team. The decisions below are how we responded to the complex project and stakeholder environment.

<Card title="1. Weekly Demo as Discovery">

Documentation was thin and cross-agency workflows weren't fully mapped. Weekly demos, shared backlog access, and named deliverables on a public roadmap let the county watch the system take shape as requirements moved.

</Card>

<Card title="2. Shared Case Model">

Four separate D365 instances would have meant rebuilding the same entity model four times plus integrations between them. One shared tenant with agency-level business units, teams, and custom security roles handled the cross-agency adjudication flow without that surface area.

</Card>

<Card title="3. Portals for Most Users">

Full CRM seats for 2,000 users would have run roughly $192K/year. Admins, investigators, and adjudicators got full CRM access. Case managers routed through D365 Portals with SAML/SSO, cutting annual licensing by roughly $150K. See the [licensing comparison](#licensing-comparison) in the appendix.

</Card>

<Card title="4. External Document Storage">

Sensitive case documents couldn't live directly in CRM, but case workers still needed to see status, signatures, and adjudication context inside D365. Government storage held the document, CRM held the case context and reference, and the integration brought signed originals back into the case workflow.

</Card>

## Act 1: Project Setup

B-LINK started before the West Region CRM practice had a mature delivery playbook. No shared tooling, no inherited engagement model, and B-LINK was the largest project the team had taken on.

I set up Azure DevOps from scratch and trained the team on how we would use it: backlog structure, sprint cadence, work-item conventions, and delivery dashboards. The delivery model was being built while the project was being delivered, and B-LINK became the testbed for how our public-sector CRM work would run.

Weekly customer demos were locked in from kickoff as the discovery vehicle for a two-year cross-agency build. Shared roadmaps, named deliverables, and tradeoff conversations ran on the same cadence.

The build work started with entity discovery, agency workflow mapping, and data mapping between county systems and state databases. Milestones built on each other from there: core case model, contact model, role-based access, state-system integrations, Portals access, and reporting.

By month eight, the cadence I'd set up was holding. The PMO came to demos engaged, tradeoffs got named in the room, and the foundation milestones kept building on each other.


## Act 2: Surprise Scope

The first real stress test came from a document-management dependency we did not control.

Perspecta's other team owned the government storage system that case documents would eventually need to flow through. We were subcontracted under Perspecta, so visibility into that team was limited. For months the dependency moved quietly, with no early signal it would become a scope issue.

We later learned that county restrictions blocked the standard SaaS e-signature path. The documents at stake carried financial, legal, and health information tied to government benefit programs, so the county required a custom certificate-based signing approach with no third-party SaaS in the chain. The signing layer had to be built from scratch.

The storage model wasn't what we'd assumed either. Documents could not live in CRM. They had to live in the government storage system Perspecta's other team owned. CRM would hold the reference and case context, the document itself would live elsewhere, and the integration had to re-surface signed originals back inside the case record for adjudication review.

Both constraints landed hardest on Portals, which required significant rework mid-engagement. I scoped the custom path with my developers and worked with PMO to defend the spend. The change order landed at roughly $600K of incremental scope, doubling the original contract.

To ship in 4 months without breaking BPAI or IHSS delivery, I pulled in engineers from other RSM regions.

The integration spanned certificate-based signing flows, secure handoff to the government storage system, signed-document resurfacing inside CRM, and a reworked Portals authorization layer threaded through every step.


## Act 3: Rollout & Adoption

- **Train-the-trainer:** One month before go-live, I trained ~20 county leaders (trainers, executives, and program managers) across two on-site sessions and weekly virtual office hours. The county's internal trainers scaled the rollout to the full 2,000 case managers. 

- **Deployment:** After validating the data mapping documents, iterating on SSIS packcages, and confirming data integrity with the client, B-LINK was approved and ready to deploy. The agencies had a parallel-run window where the legacy systems and B-LINK ran side by side against the same cases before the cutover.
