## Business Context

Big Brothers Big Sisters is the largest mentorship nonprofit in the US. Their mentor enrollment team was their biggest cost center: high call volumes, frequent turnover, and a 13-day phone-tag cycle to qualify a volunteer lead and book an interview. By the time staff reached someone, the momentum was gone.

I pitched real-time SMS as the fix. Catch volunteers while they're still motivated, automate the repetitive intake, and free staff for the relationship work of mentorship. The numbers held up: median time-to-qualification dropped from 13 days to 15 minutes, and SMS absorbed 70% of inbound volume.

I also ran a voice variant alongside SMS, built on ElevenLabs and Twilio, aimed at the older-volunteer cohort with a phone preference. I killed it after 3 days. The escalation paths were funneling callers back to the enrollment team, adding to their caseload instead of relieving it.

## System

The SMS pilot ran as part of Firstly, with Twilio handling messaging, Bubble.io powering the admin portal, and cloud functions executing the tool calls. Zapier orchestrated the Salesforce, Google Sheets, and Twilio handoffs.

![Architecture: Bubble admin portal driving Twilio Studio flows, Functions, and link tracking against the SMS audience](/images/projects/mentor-enrollment-diagram.png)

The voice variant used ElevenLabs' builder for prompts and orchestration, OpenAI for the model, and Twilio's voice channel for the calls. It ran alongside the SMS pilot without disrupting it, and we combined link-tracking metrics from SMS with voice metrics from ElevenLabs.

**Stack:**

- Twilio Studio + headless cloud functions for SMS routing and tool calls
- Firstly platform for building and managing SMS journeys, with Bubble.io admin portal
- Zapier orchestrating Salesforce, Google Sheets, and Twilio handoffs
- Google Sheets as a Salesforce integration shim (per the workaround)
- ElevenLabs voice agents + OpenAI + Twilio Voice for the voice variant
- Salesforce as system of record for volunteer inquiries

## Act 1: Discovery & Pilot

I went into the first meeting pitching SMS for mentorship scaffolding and retention. The Chief Program Officer was polite but uninterested. Halfway through the demo she stopped me: "what if we used this for mentor enrollment?" Enrollment was their biggest cost center, and she was already asking about integrations we didn't have. I knew the deal was real once those questions started.

The roadblock surfaced in the next meeting with the Enrollment Manager. Their Salesforce footprint was governed by an org-wide policy: every mentor and mentee interaction had to land in the CRM. We didn't have a Salesforce integration, and I didn't want to build integrations without validating the solution first. 

So I pitched a Google Sheet workaround. Volunteer inquiries from Salesforce would flow into the sheet via Zapier, the solution would run against the sheet, and responses would flow back to Salesforce the same way. It satisfied the policy without new integration work on either side.

We got the MOU signed and confirmed the pilot dates.

## Act 2: Shipping SMS

The SMS solution handled the repetitive front-end of enrollment. Inquiries from Salesforce triggered the flow, and from there the system:

- Acknowledged the volunteer and qualified the lead against eligibility criteria
- Routed each volunteer to the intake staff responsible for their location
- Booked the interview into the right calendar based on geography and time zone
- Wrote status back into the Salesforce integration along the way

![Inquiry-to-interview journey in the Firstly builder: opening SMS, qualification survey, and live message thread on the right](/images/projects/mentor-enrollment-journey.png)

Before the pilot started, I sat with the Enrollment Manager to map workflows, intents, scheduling rules, and escalation paths. I built a demo flow against the spec and got her sign-off.

The first month was the richest part of the engagement. Weekly check-ins with the Enrollment Manager gave us real-time read on what was landing and what was missing, and the pilot kept surfacing edge cases the spec hadn't covered. Two changes from those sessions:

- **Added:** an automatic follow-up for volunteers who started the flow but dropped before completing qualification, so warm leads got re-engaged without staff effort.
- **Scoped back:** the Enrollment Manager leaned on the Salesforce integration sheet for day-to-day visibility, which told us the admin-portal reporting wasn't an MVP feature.

The pilot worked. Median time-to-qualification dropped from 13 days to 15 minutes. SMS absorbed 70% of inbound enrollment volume.


## Act 3: Voice Experiment

Older volunteers preferred phone. The Enrollment Manager flagged a cohort she called the "empty nesters" who kept calling the office instead of using the SMS flow. They were eating the staff capacity the pilot was supposed to free up.

Voice looked like the obvious next move, and I was excited to add more agentic capabilities to our solution. I'd been prototyping with ElevenLabs agents in parallel, so I took this on to see if it was viable. I built the voice variant on ElevenLabs' agent builder, with OpenAI as the model layer and Twilio for the voice channel. It slotted into the existing SMS flow without disrupting it.

In production, the cohort assumption fell apart. The SMS flow served volunteers who submitted forms, and the voice flow served volunteers who picked up the phone. We added a fallback: if a caller hung up or the agent fumbled the flow, the system sent a follow-up SMS to recover the lead. But these were callers who preferred phone in the first place. Instead of responding to the SMS, they called the enrollment team back. The flow looked right on paper for a phone-preferring cohort, but in practice it routed inbound calls back into the staff capacity SMS was already saving.

I killed the voice variant after 3 days. The metric driving the call wasn't on the original dashboard: time-to-first-contact made both surfaces look fine, but inbound caseload to the enrollment team was the real signal, and SMS was reducing it while voice was spiking it. We reverted to SMS.


## Results

| Result | Why it mattered |
|---|---|
| 13 days → 15 minutes median time-to-qualification | The momentum hypothesis held. Volunteers self-scheduled in the same session, before the lead went cold |
| 70% of inbound enrollment volume handled by SMS | The intake team got back the capacity the pilot was designed to free |
| Voice variant killed in 3 days | Off-dashboard metric (inbound caseload to enrollment) drove the call, not the dashboard's time-to-first-contact |

## My Takeaways

- **Tie pilot value to dollars, not capacity.** I pitched this on time-to-qualification and staff capacity, which mapped to cost reduction but I never made the math explicit. Knowing what I know now about contact center economics, I'd open with an ROI model: cost per inquiry today, cost per inquiry under the SMS flow, payback period. Same pitch, sharper anchor for procurement. See [the ROI calculator I built later](https://github.com/kev-hu/vapi-voice-agent/blob/main/demo/roi-calculator.md).

- **Voice pilots need months, not weeks.** A one-week voice trial was the wrong instrument for the question. AI voice agents take time to calibrate against real conversational rhythms, and containment rates ramp slowly. If I ran this again, I'd start with high-volume, low-complexity intents on a multi-month runway, and let the team build trust with the technology before expanding scope. See [vapi-voice-agent](https://github.com/kev-hu/vapi-voice-agent) for what I'd build today.

- **What works in SMS doesn't port to voice.** SMS was working, so I took a shortcut and reused the same qualification flow, escalation paths, and fallback logic on the voice channel. The voice surface turned out to be a different system: different cohort, different intents, different failure modes. The shortcut cost us the experiment.
